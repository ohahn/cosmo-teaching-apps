Interactive cosmology

Interactive figures from the lecture course Cosmology (280416 VU), by Oliver Hahn, University of Vienna. Each one is a single web page that runs entirely in your browser: nothing to install, and no data leaves your computer.

Made for a laptop or a tablet in landscape. Every page also works offline: save it (“Save page as…”) and open the file.

Build your own universe

Pick Ωm and ΩΛ in the plane of Friedmann models and follow the expansion history a(t), the density parameters, the age, the geometry and the fate of the universe.

Light cones in an expanding Universe

Spacetime diagrams in physical distance, comoving distance and conformal time. Place an event and follow the photon it sends towards us: past light cone, horizons and cosmological redshift.

The distance zoo

Comoving, transverse, angular-diameter, luminosity and light-travel distance against redshift, and how big and how bright the same galaxy appears as it is moved to higher redshift.

Big Bang nucleosynthesis

A live nuclear reaction network for the first minutes. Change the baryon-to-photon ratio, Neff and the neutron lifetime and compare the light-element abundances with the observations.

The CMB sky and its power spectrum

Seven cosmological parameters, the TT, TE and EE spectra from a neural-network emulator of CLASS compared with Planck, and a full-sky map that morphs as the parameters change.

Simulating the cosmic web

A particle-mesh N-body simulation (FastPM) running in the browser: the cosmic web forms from Gaussian initial conditions. Zel'dovich approximation, power spectrum, light cone and redshift space.

Uses the GPU (WebGPU) where the browser supports it, otherwise the CPU.
